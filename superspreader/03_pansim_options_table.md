# ACT-049 PanSim implementation options

Options follow from the review (01_literature_review.md) and reflect secondary question (d). Citations [R#] refer to the reading list there. These options were derived from the literature and the packet text only: no PanSim code, internal documentation, ACT-004 outputs or ACT-047 results were consulted, so statements about feasibility inside PanSim are assumptions to be checked by the PanSim team.

> **Status after the phase 2 and 3 runs (see 09 and 13).** Option A (per-agent multiplier) is the only option that reached the published range of k in PanSim, and shape 0.3 (early k about 0.27 to 0.32) is the only setting that survives the wave-2 hold-out. Option B (schedules, occupancy, venue profile) is refuted on this kernel: early k stays at 3 to 5, and the density-exponent variants reach 1.8 only with an implausible venue-size distribution. A fixed-volume model (option B2, 11 and 12) moves early k from 5.2 to 3.2, and the change comes from the per-type contrast, not from fill fraction; it fails the wave-2 hold-out. Option D (explicit events) is the only route below k = 1 without the multiplier (early k 0.08 at 0.3% of social-venue days at 1000 times), but the run is not matched on early R. Identifiability: k is not a sufficient statistic for large-outbreak risk. At matched mean offspring, a caregiving profile with a hospital setting gives a thinner middle and a heavier extreme tail than a negative binomial with the same k (13, section 2).

## 2. Recommendation

Implement **C as a switchable stack**: B as the base, a modest A, and D optional. The review does not support a lifelong supershedder class for SARS-CoV-2, supports setting effects most strongly, and supports biological variation as a real but transient and weaker modifier. Starting with B only is reasonable if schedule and POI data (ACT-004) are available first, with A added as an ablation.

For Nipah the relevant factor is caregiving and health-care exposure intensity (duration, body fluids, burial), not mobility [R19, R20, R21]; use B in health-care and household settings with R set below 1 (about 0.33 in Bangladesh [R21]). For H5N1 use the same machinery as a labelled scenario with R below about 0.25 [R26, R28] and an assumed k, because none is available.

## 3. Identifiability: can behavioural, biological and setting heterogeneity be separated?

**Short answer: not from aggregate case data and a published k alone.** A single target k constrains the total variance of individual transmission, not its split. With the data normally available there are four partial handles:

1. **Independent priors for each component.** Biological variance from shedding and emission distributions [R13, R15, R16], capped by the weak viral-load to transmission gradient [R14]; behavioural variance from schedule and contact data (public time-use and contact surveys, ACT-004 output).
2. **Setting-stratified cluster data** [R6]: k and cluster sizes by setting separate setting effects from person effects only if the same person types appear across settings.
3. **Time variation:** behavioural and setting components change when restrictions change; a biological component should not. Comparing k before and after restrictions in the same population gives a weak handle (each study's data and period differ [R7]).
4. **Households as a near-homogeneous setting:** k about 1.1 in Hong Kong households [R6] suggests that personal biology alone does not generate very low k, but household size truncation inflates k and the inference is weak.

What cannot be separated with public data: a person's persistent contact propensity from their persistent shedding level, and an event's setting from the infector's emission in that event [R11].

**In simulation**, identifiability is by construction: ablation lets you quantify what each factor contributes to model-generated k, under assumed structure. That tells you how sensitive outputs are to the split, not what the split is in the real world.

**Detection matters.** Published k values come from detected clusters [R9, R10]. A simulated k must be computed after applying a comparable detection and tracing model, or the comparison is biased (experiment 3).

## 4. Dependencies

- ACT-047 (Zsófia, Hungary and healthcare-worker interventions): relevant to the Nipah health-care scenario and to calibrating baseline R in PanSim.
